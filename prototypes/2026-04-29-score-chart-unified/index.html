Connecting the Two Charts

Three solutions to the disconnection problem in the Chart + Drilldown Heatmap.

Expanding on Prototype 4 →

Approach 1

Visual Linking

Both charts share a color scale and a common axis. Hovering either chart fires a vertical guide rule that bridges the two, and the same data point lights up in both simultaneously.

Pros

  • Low cognitive load — color does the linking
  • Works even without clicking anything
  • Shared axis row makes layout feel unified

Cons

  • Passive — doesn't reward interaction
  • Guide line can feel noisy

Approach 2

Interaction Linking

A brush on the line chart dims heatmap cells outside the selected range. Clicking a heatmap cell pulses a dot on the line chart. A highlight band spans both charts for the selected date, making cause-and-effect explicit.

Pros

  • Clear cause-and-effect — one action, two responses
  • Brush enables temporal focus / range analysis
  • Highlight band is a strong visual anchor

Cons

  • Requires user to discover the interaction
  • More interaction complexity

Approach 3

Structural Unification

Replaces the two separate charts with a single layered visualization: line floats over a color strip, which sits above stacked sub-score bars. All three layers share one x-axis. Hover highlights the whole column.

Pros

  • No disconnection possible — one structure
  • Column hover reveals all data at once
  • No separate navigation needed

Cons

  • Dense — harder to scan at a glance
  • Requires more vertical space
