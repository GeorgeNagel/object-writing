Writing Progress Visualization

Four approaches to charting a single skill score (0–1) over 20 days

Approach 1

Line Chart + Rolling Average

A smooth line chart of daily scores with a 5-day rolling average overlaid as a dashed line. Hover any point for the exact score. The trend line smooths out day-to-day noise to reveal true direction.

Pros

  • Immediately readable
  • Shows raw variance and trend together
  • Easy to spot outlier sessions
  • Familiar chart pattern

Cons

  • Feels clinical, data-report-like
  • Doesn't mark milestones or celebrate progress
  • Two overlapping lines can clutter small screens

Approach 2

Score Heatmap Grid

A 4×5 grid of day-cells colored by score intensity — lighter means lower, darker means higher. Click any cell to open a detail panel with exact score, a fill bar, and context. Streaks and gaps surface instantly.

Pros

  • Streaks and consistency are instantly visible
  • Compact — fits in a card or widget
  • Click-to-drill interaction feels tactile

Cons

  • Trend direction less obvious than a line
  • Exact values require clicking each cell
  • Color encoding can be ambiguous without a legend

Approach 3

Gauge + History Strip

A large animated arc gauge shows the selected day's score. A strip of 20 bars below represents history — click any bar to animate the needle to that day's score. Milestone badges unlock as thresholds are crossed.

Pros

  • Rewarding, gamified feel
  • Score feels concrete and present-tense
  • Milestone unlocks make progress memorable

Cons

  • Trend analysis requires more scanning
  • Gauge metaphor can feel abstract
  • More complex UI for a simple metric

Approach 4

Chart + Drilldown Heatmap

A line chart on top for overall trend, paired with a blue-to-red heatmap below for session navigation. Click any cell to drill into eight per-sense sub-scores (words, sight, hearing, touch, kinaesthetic, organic, smell), each shown as an animated bar.

Pros

  • Combines trend and session detail in one view
  • Sub-score bars reveal what drove the overall score
  • Blue-red scale immediately signals high vs. low
  • Scales naturally to more dimensions

Cons

  • More visual weight than the other approaches
  • Requires real sub-score data from the backend
  • Heatmap and chart can feel disconnected
